About

Runtian Wu

PhD student · Molecular Biology · University of California, Los Angeles

I study how plants remember — and what their memories are made of.

At the Jacobsen Lab, I work with a small weed called Arabidopsis thaliana — a plant with no flowers worth photographing and no fruit worth eating, but with an outsized talent for chemically annotating its own genome. My research asks how these annotations — methyl groups written quietly onto DNA — are established, inherited, interpreted, and erased.

To find out, I sequence methylomes to follow millions of modified cytosines, map where proteins sit across the genome, use mass spectrometry to uncover the company they keep, and read transcriptomes to see what changes when the system is disturbed. I like the seam between bench and computer most — where a wet-lab observation becomes a hypothesis you can test overnight, or where a pattern buried in millions of sequencing reads tells you what experiment to do next.
